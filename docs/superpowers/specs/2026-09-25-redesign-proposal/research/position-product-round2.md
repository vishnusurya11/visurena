## 2. HOLD

1. **UX: "Home stays browse-first until ~5 series."** With one *complete* series, the hero rotator's second slot must be Frankenstein (in production, Follow CTA). A viewer who finished 14/14 finds no other news on the page; one demand element in the fold is programming, not a feature.
2. **UX: a browse hub behind Watch on mobile.** With one series, a hub is a page whose only job is to be tapped through. Watch opens the feed scoped to the series; the hub arrives with series three.
3. **Everyone: the end of a complete series is a dead end in every plan.** After Ep 14 ends, and at the foot of the episode list, the card is "You pick what we make next": top three requests plus Follow. It is the last retention moment a finished catalogue has.

## 3. VOTE

**Cinema Lobby.** A lit one-sheet whose floor line doubles as the series progress bar puts programming state (Ep 9 of 14, Complete, In production) into the artwork itself, which is what a thin catalogue needs: few posters, each carrying its own status. **Kill Signal**: it turns the Request board into Canny with a ticker, the exact failure mode; Broadsheet demands weekly editorial copy a one-person studio will not sustain.

## 4. THE ONE THING

Every work on every surface carries a state line with a **date or a count**: "Ep 9 of 14 · next Thu", "Complete · 14 episodes", "In production · Ep 1 rendering", "412 votes". One "Coming soon" with neither, and the redesign failed.
